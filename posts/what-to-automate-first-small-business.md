Start with the thing you do every week without thinking.

Not your biggest problem. Your most repeated one. It adds up over a year, and it is usually the easiest kind of task to hand over to a system.

## The sticky note test

Here is a quick way to tell if a task can be automated. If you could write the steps on a sticky note, and a new person could follow them without asking you anything, it can be automated.

If the steps change every time because it needs a judgement call, like calming down an upset client, it is a job for a person. That is fine. The aim is to free up your time for those jobs.

## Four tasks that are usually first in line

**Invoices and reminders.** Make the invoice, send it, notice when it is late, send a nudge. Every step is the same each time, and forgetting one costs you money.

**Bookings.** Going back and forth on WhatsApp to find a time works until two people want the same slot. A booking page lets clients pick a time and you confirm it.

**Follow-ups.** The call you meant to make, the quote you meant to chase. A list that shows who is due today replaces a sticky note and your memory.

**Weekly numbers.** If you rebuild the same report from the same sheet every Monday, that is a task a system can do for you.

## How to pick yours

Try this for one week. Every time you do a task you have done before, write it down. At the end of the week, circle the ones that:

1. take you more than 15 minutes, or
2. cost you something when you forget, like a late invoice or a lost client.

Start with a task that does both.

## Don't automate a mess

If a process is confusing when you do it by hand, it will be confusing when it is automated, only faster. Write down the steps first. Sometimes you will spot a step you can cut, and then you don't need the automation at all.

Then do one task at a time. When the first one runs without you thinking about it, pick the next.
